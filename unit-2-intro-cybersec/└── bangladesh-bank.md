1. What happened: Hackers tried to steal nearly $1 billion from Bangladesh Bank using fake transfer requests. They managed to grab $81 million before getting cut off, hiding the cash in Philippine casinos.

2. Taking their time: They hung out inside the bank's computer network for almost a year. This gave them time to steal passwords, figure out how things worked, and learn employee schedules.

 3. Messing with data: They didn't lock people out or steal secrets; instead, they secretly tweaked transaction data so their fake transfers looked 100% legit to the Federal Reserve.

4. The printer trick: They hit the bank with a virus that disabled the physical printer. When warning messages came in over the weekend, no physical papers printed out, buying the hackers precious time to move the money.

5. Where they messed up: A typo in one of the transfer names flagged a manual review, safety checks caught other flags, and workers eventually showed up on Monday to catch the printer backlog.

6. How to fix it: Network separation. If the bank had kept its high-security money computers totally separate from ordinary office computers, the hackers' virus never could have crossed over.

7. The big picture: Cyberattacks aren't just for spying or ransomware anymore—they are used for massive bank heists. Banks have to treat their software just like physical vaults.

8. Final thought: It shows how fragile global banking is. One weak computer link can put money at risk halfway across the world.
