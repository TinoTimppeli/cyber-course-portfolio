1. The incident in my own words

WannaCry was a big cyberattack that happened in 2017. It locked people's files and then asked them to pay money. It spread extremely quickly and affected computers in over 150 countries, including hospitals, companies and government offices.

2. Who was affected, and how

A lot of normal people and organisations were affected. Hospitals were one of the biggest problems because staff could not use some computer systems, which caused delays for patients. Businesses also lost time and money because their computers were suddenly useless.

3. The CIA principle

The main problem was Availability. The ransomware stopped people from accessing their files and systems. Confidentiality could also be affected, but WannaCry was mainly about locking people out of their own computers.

4. The attack technique

Ransomware is malware that locks your files and demands money. WannaCry was different because it could spread through networks without someone having to click a link. It was basically like one infected computer saying, hey you should get and infection too hahahahah.

5. How it was discovered and stopped

The attack became obvious when people started seeing ransom messages and losing access to their computers. A researcher found a strange website address inside the malware and registered it. This unexpectedly stopped WannaCry from spreading further, which was a pretty lucky break.

6. What could have helped

Microsoft had already released a fix for the Windows weakness, but many organisations had not installed it. Some systems were old or difficult to update. Good backups would also have helped because people could restore their files instead of paying the attackers.

7. The broader lesson

WannaCry showed that ignoring updates can cause much bigger problems than just having an annoying update notification. It also showed that hospitals and other important services need to take old systems seriously. Another lesson was that powerful hacking tools can cause real damage when they escape and are used by criminals.
