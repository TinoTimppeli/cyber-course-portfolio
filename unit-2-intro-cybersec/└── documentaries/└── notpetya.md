1. The incident in my own words

NotPetya happened in 2017 and started with a Ukrainian accounting program called M.E.Doc. An infected update got onto people's computers and the virus started moving through their networks. It was meant to cause trouble in Ukraine, but it ended up spreading much further.

2. Who was affected, and how

Big companies like Maersk were badly hit. Maersk lost access to many computers and had to shut down parts of its business while fixing everything. The total damage from NotPetya was estimated at billions of dollars.

3. The CIA principle

I would say Availability was the main problem. Computers and files were made unusable, so workers could not do their jobs. It asked for money like ransomware, but the files were not really going to be recovered, so the main point was damage.

4. The attack technique

The attackers got into M.E.Doc and used its normal update to spread the malware. People installed the update because they trusted the program. This shows why software updates can be risky if the software company has been hacked.

5. How it spread inside networks

NotPetya used a Windows weakness to move between computers. It could also grab passwords and use them to get into other machines. So even computers that had been patched could sometimes still be reached.

6. What could have helped

Keeping backups away from the main network would have helped. Even if the computers were destroyed, the company could use the backup to get important files back.

7. The broader lesson

NotPetya showed that a cyberattack does not always stay where it starts. An attack aimed at Ukraine ended up hurting companies in many other countries. It also showed how cyberattacks can be used as part of problems between countries.

8. My personal takeaway

What stood out to me was how one trusted program caused such a huge mess. I will also take backups more seriously because losing everything at once would be a nightmare.
