1. What happened: Dutch hackers used an old leaked password to break into a presidential candidate's Twitter account.

2. Credential reuse: This happens when you use the same password on multiple websites. One old leak puts all your other accounts at risk.

3. Confidentiality: The attack broke confidentiality because strangers gained private access to an account they shouldn't have been in.

4. Credential stuffing: Hackers take leaked password lists and test them on different websites until they find one that works.

5. Simple flaw, big target: Even a super important person can get hacked if they have poor personal password habits.

6. How to fix it: Use a password manager for unique passwords and turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA).

7. Leaked data is forever: Old data stays online for years. Waiting until something bad happens to change your password is way too late.

8. Your action: Check if your email is on HaveIBeenPwned and set up strong passwords and MFA this week.
