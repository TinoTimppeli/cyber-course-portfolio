1. What Happened
In 2012, hackers attacked Saudi Aramco, a massive oil company. They used a bad file from an email to get inside the computer network. A hidden virus then wiped out 35,000 computers completely, destroying all their files and operating systems.

2. Who Was Hurt?
The company produces a huge amount of the worlds oil. Even though the actual oil drilling pumps kept working, all the office computers stopped. Workers could not use email, send bills, track shipments, or talk to customers.

3. The CIA Triad
The attack ruined system availability and data integrity. Unlike stealing data, a wiper completely deletes everything. This means the company had to replace physical parts instead of just fixing software.

4. How It Spread
The company had a flat network, meaning everything was connected in one big group without inner walls. This let the virus spread easily to thousands of computers. Unlike ransomware that wants money to unlock files, a wiper destroys them completely just to cause chaos.

5. How They Fixed It
The company unplugged from the internet to stop the spread and flew private planes to buy every hard drive they could find. This shows that having emergency plans and backup parts costs a lot of money, but you need it when disaster strikes.

6. Better Defenses
Dividing the network into smaller, separate zones would have helped a lot. If one part got infected, those walls would stop the virus from reaching the rest of the computers.

7. The Big Lesson
This attack shows that vital places like energy companies, hospitals, and transport are targets for sabotage. Attacks on these important services can cause global panic and harm society.

8. My Takeaway
thhis shows why relying only on computers without offline paper backups is risky, Without offline emergency contact lists and plans, a company is completely lost when technology breaks down.
